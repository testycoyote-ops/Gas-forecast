How the forecast works

One equation, no black box.

The AAA national average is a survey of ~120,000 stations, so it is heavily smoothed and moves in runs rather than jumps. That makes today's move the single best predictor of tomorrow's move:

changetomorrow = drift + 0.871 × (changetoday − drift)

where drift is the average daily change over the past week. In plain terms: tomorrow repeats about 87% of today's move, pulled slightly back toward the week's pace.

*Walk-forward out-of-sample test on 1,756 weeks (1992–2026) of EIA weekly US regular retail prices, expanding window, coefficient re-estimated at every step. The 0.871 daily φ is derived from the weekly autocorrelation (0.548) by temporal aggregation; that weekly figure is stable at 0.532–0.548 across every sub-era tested, so the daily φ lands in a tight 0.864–0.871 band.

Known limits. φ started as an inference from weekly EIA data rather than a fit on a long daily AAA series, and it is still mostly that: daily changes are small and noisy, so it takes a great deal of data to pin down. The ± band is the model's own realised forecast error, shrunk toward its prior — treat it as approximate while the case count is low. Against the Kalshi ladder the model has been quoting a distribution several times too wide, and it loses to the market on Brier score. The model is pure momentum: it will be wrong on the day a hurricane, refinery outage, or geopolitical shock hits, because it does not read the news.
